# Business Insights

## 1. Repeat Customers Drive Revenue

### Key Finding

Repeat customers generated **$3.42M**, representing **99.38% of total sales**.

### Evidence

* Repeat Customers: 9,500
* Repeat Orders: 48,878
* Average Orders per Customer: 5.15
* Average Revenue per Customer: $360.48

### Business Implication

The business relies heavily on existing customers as its primary source of revenue.

### Recommendation

Focus on customer retention and repeat purchases through loyalty programs, personalized offers, and targeted campaigns.

> Note: The 99.38% figure represents sales contribution from repeat customers, not customer retention rate.

---

## 2. Electronics Is the Main Revenue Driver

### Key Finding

**Electronics generated $1.75M**, accounting for **50.83% of total sales**.

### Evidence

Electronics was the highest-performing category, followed by Accessories at 16.05%.

### Business Implication

Electronics is the main contributor to overall revenue and has a significant impact on business performance.

### Recommendation

Maintain product availability in Electronics and consider cross-selling complementary products to increase order value.

---

## 3. Sales Show a Downward Trend

### Key Finding

Sales decreased slightly in 2025 and declined further in the first half of 2026.

### Evidence

* 2024 Sales: $1.46M
* 2025 Sales: $1.44M
* 2025 YoY Change: -1.07%
* Jan–Jun 2026 vs Jan–Jun 2025: -24.80%

### Business Implication

The decline suggests that sales performance should be monitored closely, particularly during the first half of 2026.

### Recommendation

Investigate changes in product demand, customer activity, and monthly sales patterns to identify potential factors behind the decline.

> Note: 2026 contains data only from January to June, so it should not be compared directly with full-year sales from 2024 or 2025.

---

## 4. Top 10 Products Contribute Most of the Revenue

### Key Finding

The **Top 10 products generated 70.71% of total sales**.

### Evidence

* Top 10 Product Sales: $2.44M
* Total Sales: $3.45M
* Revenue Contribution: 70.71%

### Business Implication

Revenue is concentrated among a relatively small group of products.

### Recommendation

Monitor inventory and availability of top-performing products while developing other products to diversify revenue sources.

---

## 5. High Sales Volume Does Not Always Mean High Revenue

### Key Finding

Products with the highest sales quantity are not necessarily the products generating the highest revenue.

### Evidence

* Notebook: 10,334 units → $67,237 sales
* Headphones: 4,804 units → $333,531 sales
* Tablet: 1,228 units → $292,344 sales

### Business Implication

Product performance should not be evaluated using sales quantity alone.

### Recommendation

Use multiple metrics such as **Sales, Quantity, and Average Order Value (AOV)** when evaluating product performance.

---

## 6. Higher Discounts Are Associated With Lower AOV

### Key Finding

Orders with higher discount levels showed lower average order values.

### Evidence

* 0% Discount → AOV: $74.83
* 30% Discount → AOV: $55.76

### Business Implication

Higher discount levels are associated with lower AOV in this dataset.

### Recommendation

Analyze whether each discount level generates sufficient sales value to justify the discount before applying larger discounts.

> Note: This analysis shows an association between discount level and AOV. It does not prove that higher discounts directly cause lower AOV.
